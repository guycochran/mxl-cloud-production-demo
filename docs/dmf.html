Context

The EBU Dynamic Media Facility

MXL doesn't exist in a vacuum — it's the data plane of a bigger idea called the Dynamic Media Facility (DMF), the EBU's reference architecture for turning a broadcast plant from a room full of boxes into software. Understanding the DMF is the fastest way to understand why MXL is shaped the way it is.

Office Hours GlobalSourced from tech.ebu.chUpdated 2026-10-01

What the DMF is

The EBU's own description of the working group's mission: it "investigates and defines architectures, workflows and operational practices that enable media facilities to transition from fixed, hardware-centric environments to flexible, software-defined infrastructures."

That's the whole thesis in one sentence. The traditional facility is fixed in metal — boxes and cabling. The DMF asks what it looks like when the facility is composable software on general-purpose compute, and defines the architecture to get there. MXL is the piece that moves the media between the software functions. Why that shift matters →

Who's behind it

The DMF is an EBU initiative, co-chaired by Peter Brightwell (BBC) and Félix Poulin (CBC/Radio-Canada). In Q3 2025 the EBU and AMWA formed a joint task force (JT-DMF) to align the work — significant because it brings the NMOS control-plane standards body (AMWA) and the European broadcasting union (EBU) under one roof for this architecture. Source: EBU DMF group and the JT-DMF announcement.

That governance matters for one practical reason: it's why MXL, TAMS and NMOS are designed to fit together rather than compete — they're being shepherded by the same coalition.

The reference-architecture white paper

The DMF is documented in a white paper, "The Dynamic Media Facility: Reference Architecture" — first published September 2024, with a v2.0 released in mid-April 2026. It's the canonical statement of the architecture: the layers, the roles, and how media functions, a media plane, storage and control fit together. Source: EBU white paper.

How MXL, TAMS and NMOS fit

The DMF is an architecture; these are the concrete open specs filling its layers:

LayerSpecRole
Media plane (data)MXLMoves media between software functions via shared memory (zero-copy) and, across hosts, a fabric.
StorageTAMSTime-addressable media store (BBC) — record and retrieve media by timerange.
Control planeAMWA NMOS (incl. BCP-007-03)Discovery, connection and routing; BCP-007-03 makes MXL flows controllable by standard NMOS controllers.

A useful mental model: NMOS is how you wire it, MXL is how the media flows, TAMS is where it lands. The architecture page goes deep on the MXL layer; the reading path orders all of it for a newcomer.

Where it is now

The vision has shipped its first concrete pieces. MXL reached a stable production release (v1.0.0 moved from release-candidate to stable after ~15 months of development), and in 2026 it was named a finalist — and won — the IBC 2026 Innovation Award, Content Creation category. Multiple vendors now ship native MXL support. Sources: EBU — MXL v1.0.0; EBU — IBC2026 finalist.

The DMF is no longer just a paper — it's an architecture with running code, a standards coalition, and a shelf of products. See who's building on it → or what it's like to actually run it →.
