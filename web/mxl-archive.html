EBU MXL · BBC TAMS · Azure · WebRTC

A broadcast switcher that lived in the cloud and ran in a browser.

For a few days in September 2026, every frame on this page crossed an MXL shared-memory domain running in Azure — cut live, clipped frame-accurate, and delivered to a browser over WebRTC. No SDI. No proprietary codecs in the middle. Just open standards. This is its archive.

01 What it did

Three broadcast pillars — switch, clip, distribute — all browser-native, all on open standards, all running on Linux VMs in Azure.

Switch · liveCut the show from anywhere

A full production switcher in the browser: preview/program, a keyer with SMPTE test patterns and lower-thirds, 2-UP / PiP / 4-UP layouts, and an audio mixer. Sources live natively in the MXL domain as uncompressed 1080p30 v210 — the switch is a pointer flip in shared memory, not a re-encode.

Clip · frame-accurateGrab a highlight, export a Short

A browser NLE reading directly from a BBC TAMS time-addressable store: scrub by timecode, mark in/out on the frame, and export a frame-exact clip with audio — assembled in-browser with ffmpeg.wasm. One toggle turns any moment into a vertical 1080×1920 YouTube Short.

Distribute · monitorSee the whole plant at a glance

A live health wall for every flow, VM and tunnel — grain rates, fabric throughput, decode integrity — so a one-day event can run unattended. The full TAMS editor sits alongside for deep review of the recorded timeline.
